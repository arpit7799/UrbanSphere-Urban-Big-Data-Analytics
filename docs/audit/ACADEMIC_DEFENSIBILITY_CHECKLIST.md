- **Defensible Action & Answer:**

We identified this discrepancy during quality auditing, corrected the UTC timestamps to IST (+5:30), and synchronized the hourly indices across all three years."

- **Defensibility Rating:** **CURRENTLY INDEFENSIBLE WITHOUT CORRECTION**
- **Vulnerability / Trap:** An examiner identifies that surface pressure was concatenated with mean sea level pressure (MSLP).
- **Defensible Answer:**
  > "Gurugram is situated at an elevation of approximately 230 meters above sea level. The Open-Meteo reanalysis dataset provided true surface pressure (~982 hPa), whereas the 2024 observational dataset reported Mean Sea Level Pressure (MSLP, ~1015 hPa). We resolved this physical unit mismatch by converting surface pressure to MSLP using the barometric elevation formula, ensuring continuous physical compatibility."

### Question 5: "How many air quality stations do you have, where are they located, and why does your cleaned dataset have 1,979 rows instead of a multiple of 365?"
- **Current Evidence in Repository:**
  - 1,464 rows in 2024 across 4 stations (366 days * 4 stations).
  - 515 rows in 2022–2023 from a single virtual reanalysis point (150 days in 2022 + 365 days in 2023).
  - 215 days missing from 2022.
- **Defensibility Rating:** **FULLY DEFENSIBLE (EXACT NUMBERS VERIFIED)**
- **Defensible Answer:**
  > "In 2024, we have exactly 1,464 records representing the four official CPCB CAAQMS stations in Gurugram (Vikas Sadan in Sector 11, Sector 51 IMD, Teri Gram, and NISE Gwal Pahari) over the 366 days of the leap year. For 2022 and 2023, we supplemented this with 515 daily records from the Open-Meteo CAMS atmospheric reanalysis model. The first 215 days of 2022 (January 1 to August 3) were omitted because the satellite reanalysis model had null outputs for those dates. Thus: 1,464 observed + 515 reanalysis = 1,979 total records."

- **Defensibility Rating:** **FULLY DEFENSIBLE IF ARCHITECTURAL SEPARATION IS MAINTAINED**
- **Defensible Answer:**
  > "We deliberately avoided joining 2018 traffic with 2024 weather on timestamps. Gurugram experienced substantial urban transformation between 2018 and 2024, including the commissioning of the Dwarka Expressway and post-pandemic mobility shifts. Correlating 2018 traffic with 2024 air quality would commit an ecological fallacy. Instead, we maintain separate analytical datasets: a dynamic time-series pipeline for 2024 Weather and Air Quality, and a static spatial profiling pipeline for 2018 corridor capacity and vehicle compositions."
